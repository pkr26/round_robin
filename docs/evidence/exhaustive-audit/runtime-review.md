## Audit runner interruption follow-up

Stopping a current full-audit attempt exposed a separate lifecycle defect: sending SIGINT only to `audit.py` let `subprocess.run` stop its immediate launcher while the verifier and tests continued after the coverage-capture lock was released. This is a newly observed failure during this audit, not an inherited historical finding.

`scripts/audit.py` now starts each command in an owned process group, defers an interrupt until a new process has been assigned, and restores the caller's signal handlers after cleanup. It first sends SIGINT and gives the verifier 45 seconds to clean up its service sessions and disposable infrastructure, then escalates to TERM and KILL with five-second waits. Repeated interruption cannot skip cleanup. A launcher that exits while its group remains alive fails verification even if its exit status was zero. The failed record is persisted before releasing the capture lock.

The expanded 49-case runner suite passed with 100% statement coverage (138/138) and 100% branch coverage (40/40), without exclusions. Fresh artifacts and retained coverage shards are in `.local/exhaustive-audit/runtime/runner-lifecycle-final/`. Real local-process regressions signal only the audit process with SIGINT or SIGTERM, hold a grandchild in cleanup, verify the capture lock remains held, send a repeated interrupt, then confirm the launcher and grandchild are gone while an unrelated process survives. Additional actual-process cases force TERM, force KILL, and reject a successful early launcher exit. Failure tests exercise spawn, wait, cleanup and final-record errors. An isolated replay restoring only the former `subprocess.run` command behavior fails all three real interruption regressions; its log is `.local/exhaustive-audit/runtime/runner-lifecycle-before-replay/tests.log`. This replay is a focused behavioral comparison, not a complete earlier-source snapshot.

The evidence and visual reviewers independently inspected process ownership, signal handling, escalation and lock lifetime. No further concrete defect was found. SIGKILL cannot execute application cleanup, and an OS process that cannot terminate within the bounded cleanup window produces a failed audit; this test evidence does not claim recovery from an unresponsive kernel or infrastructure daemon. Final repository-wide results remain the responsibility of the clean root verification run.

## Limits

SQLite and synthetic-provider tests do not establish distributed PostgreSQL behavior, actual provider billing, hosted deployment safety or useful model conclusions. Native-service, TLS, browser and final strict service-coverage results are recorded by the root verifier. An absent supported cache tariff intentionally leaves exposure unknown until evidenced reconciliation. Persistent artifact failure retains authorized/unknown exposure and marks recovery required; it does not silently charge zero or resend. No coverage exclusion or denominator reduction was added for this work. Unreachable defensive fallthroughs remain in production code and the measured denominator.

## Final process-group portability correction

A full rerun reproduced an intermittent Darwin `EPERM` while an owned process group was becoming zombie-only. Apple XNU filters zombies when checking signal permission, so a permission error is not proof that the group has disappeared. Probes now treat it as potentially live and continue bounded reaping; denied signals wait for proven disappearance, then fail with the original denial if the deadline expires. No ownership, lock or interruption-record assertions were weakened. Three new deterministic/actual-zombie regressions fail against the preceding source in an isolated replay. All **52 runner tests pass**; final focused coverage is **144/144 statements and 42/42 branches**. A separate **1,500-owned-child exit probe** completed and reaped every child. Root independently re-read the final implementation. Final all-service acceptance remains in REPORT.md.
